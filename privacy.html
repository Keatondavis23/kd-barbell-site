KD Barbell privacy policy

Last updated: November 2, 2026 (version 0.41: an account is optional — see Using the app without an account; version 0.40: lab records are no longer shown or added — see Lab results from earlier versions). Applies to the KD Barbell Android app (package com.fitledger.fitledger) published by KD Barbell, and to this website. Email and password sign-in is available in v0.39.0 and later.

In short. KD Barbell is a personal log. It stores only what you put into it or ask it to read, on your phone and — if you sign in — in your own private space in the app's cloud project. An account is optional: you can use the app without one, and your records then stay on your phone and are not sent anywhere. Signing in — with a Google account, or with an email address and a password — is how records are backed up and synced. Nothing is sold, shared with other users, or used for advertising; the app has no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software. You can export everything, delete records while keeping the account, or delete the account and all of its data inside the app or by email.

Who we are and what this covers

KD Barbell is published by KD Barbell (the "we" in this policy). Contact: nikelunch2000@gmail.com. This policy covers the Android app and the pages of this website. It describes the app as it is built today; if the app changes in a way that affects your data, this page is updated and the date above changes.

What the app records

The app records only what you enter, import or allow it to read. Depending on how you use it, that is:

  • Training records — exercises (including ones you create), workouts, sets (weight and repetitions or seconds), cardio sessions, programs and saved workouts.
  • Body and fitness records — body weight and body-fat figures, tape measurements, injuries and pain check-ins, nutrition, sleep and daily check-ins (such as steps, heart rate and blood pressure), and goals. Every value is kept and shown as you recorded it; the app attaches no category, reference range or interpretation to it.
  • Lab results from earlier versions — if you recorded lab markers, draws and results in a version before 0.40, that data is still part of your records (see below). Version 0.40 and later have no screen for it and cannot add to it.
  • Records imported from files you choose — a Cronometer nutrition export, a JEFIT workout export or a Samsung Health export (steps). The file is read on your phone and turned into records; the file itself is not uploaded. Each imported value keeps a note of where it came from, and the app stores import bookkeeping (the source record's identifier and a fingerprint of its values) so that importing the same file again does not duplicate anything.
  • Health Connect data — only if you turn it on and grant the Android permissions: daily nutrition totals, step counts, heart-rate and resting-heart-rate figures and sleep sessions, from the apps you choose. See Health Connect.
  • Your account — if you sign in with Google: the email address, display name and Google user identifier of that account. If you create an account with an email address and password: the address, the optional name you type, and your password, which is sent securely to Firebase Authentication and stored there as a salted hash. The app holds the password temporarily while you enter it; it does not save it in the records database, settings, backups or logs. In both cases Firebase assigns an account identifier (UID). The app shows your name or email in Account & sync and stores the email on the phone to remember which account the phone's copy belongs to. The app does not request access to anything else in a Google account.
  • An optional profile picture — an image you pick through the Android file picker, resized to 512 pixels and stored as a setting. The app does not request access to your photo library.
  • Settings — units, goals, the layout of the Today page, which check-in fields you use, rest-timer preferences and similar choices.
  • Sync bookkeeping — a random installation identifier the app generates the first time it syncs on a phone, written into each synced record so that your phones can tell which one made a change; revision counters and timestamps. This identifier is not derived from your device and is not shared with anyone outside the sync.

The app does not collect your location, contacts, calendar, messages, call logs, microphone or camera input, browsing history, or an advertising identifier. It has no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software, and it sends no usage statistics.

Signing in

Sign-in uses Firebase Authentication, operated by Google, in one of two ways. With Google: Google verifies your account and provides the app with your email address, display name, Google user identifier and profile-photo address. With an email address and password: you create the account in the app; Firebase stores the address and a salted hash of the password, sends you a verification email once (the account opens after the link in it has been used), and sends a password-reset email when you ask for one from Forgot password? — the app itself sends no mail. A Google account can later add a password from Account & sync; it stays the same account with the same records. Either way Firebase Authentication keeps a user record with that information and the sign-in times. Google states that Firebase Authentication also uses IP addresses and user-agent strings to protect sign-in against abuse, keeps logged IP addresses for a few weeks, retains other authentication information (including passwords, as hashes) until the developer deletes the user, and removes it from live and backup systems within 180 days after that. Firebase Authentication runs from data centers in the United States. Google's handling of Google sign-in is described in the Google Privacy Policy; Firebase's in Privacy and Security in Firebase.

Since version 0.41 signing in is optional (see Using the app without an account). When you do sign in, the records of an account are kept apart from every other account's — and from the records kept without an account — and the app refuses to show one account's records to another. Two accounts are never merged because they use the same email address.

Using the app without an account

Since version 0.41 the first screen offers Continue without an account. The app then keeps your records in a separate database in its private storage on your phone and nowhere else: no account is created (not an anonymous one either), nothing is uploaded or synced, and no Firebase service is contacted for those records. The app shows Saved on this phone where it would otherwise show your account, and Sign in to back up and sync your records as the one way to change that. Health Connect data you choose to read in this mode is stored in the same on-phone database, with its own consent, and is likewise never sent anywhere.

Records kept without an account are yours to keep or delete: export them as a file at any time (Profile › Export data), or delete them with Delete guest records. Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage removes them, because the phone is the only place they exist — the app says so, and the export is the way to keep a copy.

If you sign in later, nothing moves by itself. Once the account is ready the app asks once what should happen to the records you kept without an account: keep them on this phone (the default — they stay as they are, apart from the account, and are shown again when you continue without an account after signing out) or copy them into the account, which adds a copy of them to the account's records and syncs it like any other record of that account. The records on the phone stay in both cases until you delete them.

Where your data is kept

On your phone

The working copy of your records is a database in the app's private storage on your phone; records kept without an account are a second such database beside it. Android's automatic app backup is turned off for KD Barbell, so Android does not copy these databases to your Google account. An account's copy stays on the phone until you delete records, delete the account, sign out (which removes the phone's copy after syncing it) or uninstall the app; the records kept without an account stay until you delete them or uninstall the app. The app does not keep a separate cloud cache; the phone databases are the only local copies.

In the cloud

When you are signed in, your account's records are synced to Cloud Firestore, a Google Cloud database in the app's Firebase project, under a path that belongs to your account alone (users/<your UID>/…). Server-side security rules allow a signed-in account to read and write only its own path. Data travels between the phone and Google over encrypted (TLS) connections, and Google encrypts it at rest. Cloud Firestore stores the data in the Google Cloud location that was fixed when the project was created; Google may replicate it within that location for durability. The sync exists so that the same account can restore its records on another phone and so that two of your phones stay in step; the app never reads or writes outside your own path, and viewing records does not write anything to the cloud.

Exports you make

Profile › Export data writes a complete copy of your records to a location you choose through the Android file picker. That file is yours; the app does not keep or upload it.

Who processes your data

Google is the only service provider. It operates Firebase Authentication and Google Sign-In (identity, the verification and password-reset emails), and Cloud Firestore (storage of your synced records) on our behalf under Google's Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms. Google also processes the technical information that any online service receives, such as the IP address and request details of each connection, as described in its own terms. We do not sell your data, share it with other users, give it to advertisers or data brokers, or use it for research or profiling. We do not use it to show you advertising. We will disclose data only if the law requires it.

Health Connect

Health Connect is Android's shared health data store on your phone. KD Barbell can read four categories from it, each only after you turn it on under Data sources, confirm the in-app consent (which identifies this phone and, when signed in, the account that will receive the records) and grant the Android permission: nutrition totals (READ_NUTRITION), steps (READ_STEPS), heart rate and resting heart rate (READ_HEART_RATE, READ_RESTING_HEART_RATE) and sleep sessions (READ_SLEEP). With the optional history permission (READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY) an import can reach further back than the last 30 days. The app never writes to Health Connect.

What it reads becomes your own records: shown to you in Nutrition, Vitals and Sleep and in their charts, stored on your phone and, when signed in, synced to your own account space exactly like the records you type in. In guest mode, the imported records stay on your phone; they reach an account only if you later explicitly choose to copy them into it. Health Connect data is not used for advertising, is not sold, is not shared with anyone, and is not used to make decisions about you. You can turn each category off in the app, or revoke the permissions in Health Connect, at any time; records already imported stay in your log until you delete them. Health Connect's own store belongs to Android and the apps that write to it; it is not changed by anything KD Barbell does.

Lab results from earlier versions

Until version 0.39 the app had a Labs section: markers with units and reference ranges, dated draws and results. Version 0.40 removed that section and every interpretation of readings from the app. Nothing was deleted by the change: lab markers, draws and results you recorded earlier stay in the database on your phone, in your account's cloud space (so they still sync between your phones and are restored on a new one), in the complete-copy backup and in the spreadsheet export (lab_results.csv). They are not shown anywhere in the app, no new lab records can be created, and no reference range or guidance is attached to them any more. You can get them out with Profile › Export data, and you remove them with everything else through Profile › Delete all records or by deleting your account.

Your choices and controls

  • Export a complete copy of your records at any time (Profile › Export data).
  • Delete individual records wherever they are shown, or a whole workout from its menu.
  • Delete all records while keeping the account: Profile › Delete all records removes every record and setting on the phone and asks whether the cloud copy should go too. Keeping the cloud copy means the next sync brings everything back; deleting it removes your synced data at the next sync.
  • Delete the account and everything in it: Profile › Account & sync › Delete my account and cloud data, or by email. See Delete your account. Records kept without an account are not part of the account and are not affected.
  • Delete the records kept without an account: Delete guest records on the guest Account & sync page or under Profile, or from a signed-in account's Account & sync page. Nothing is deleted anywhere but on the phone, because nothing else exists.
  • Sign out: your records are synced first, then the phone's copy is removed; they come back when you sign in again.
  • Health Connect: turn categories off in Data sources, or revoke the permissions in Health Connect.
  • Troubleshooting summary: Account & sync › Help › Troubleshooting can copy a short status text to your clipboard for a support message. It contains the app version, platform, and sync and Health Connect status with sanitized error codes, and deliberately no email address, identifier, record count or health value. Nothing is sent unless you paste it into a message yourself.

How long data is kept

  • Your records stay until you delete them. When you delete records, the account, or the cloud copy, the app deletes the corresponding documents from Cloud Firestore and the Firebase user record where applicable. Google states that Cloud Firestore data deleted by a customer is removed from its active systems and then from its backups, with a maximum of about 180 days, as described in Data deletion on Google Cloud. We hold no other copy.
  • The Firebase user record (email, name, photo address for Google accounts, the password hash for email accounts, identifiers, sign-in times) is kept until the account is deleted, after which Google removes it within 180 days. IP addresses logged by Firebase Authentication are kept by Google for a few weeks.
  • The copy on your phone is kept until you delete records, delete the account, sign out or uninstall the app. Records kept without an account are kept on the phone until you delete them or uninstall the app; we never hold them.
  • Exports you made are files under your control; the app does not keep them.
  • Support and deletion emails you send us, and our replies, are kept in our support mailbox for up to 12 months after the matter is closed, as a record that it was handled, and then deleted. You can ask us to delete the correspondence earlier.

Security

Data moves between the app and Google over TLS; Google encrypts stored data at rest by default. Cloud Firestore security rules limit every signed-in account to its own path, and the app checks the signed-in account before every write. The app has not undergone an independent security audit or certification, and no system is perfectly secure; please use a Google account with 2-Step Verification or a long, unique password, and keep your phone locked.

Children

KD Barbell is intended for adults. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18. If you believe a child has created an account, email us and we will delete it.
